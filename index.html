Your backups have become a junk drawer.

Old laptops. Phone dumps. Three external drives that all contain a folder called “Photos”. Somewhere in there is the only copy of something you love, buried under a dozen copies of everything else.

Sorting it by hand would take weeks. Handing it to a black-box tool feels worse. Undupe takes the middle path: it does the heavy lifting, shows its work, and asks before it acts.

Features

Exact matches only

Files are compared by SHA-256 fingerprints, never by name, date or size alone. If Undupe says two files are duplicates, they are identical, byte for byte. Most files are ruled out early, so they never get read in full.

FAQ

Good questions.

Does it upload my files?
No. Everything runs locally in your browser, and there is no backend. After the page loads, Undupe makes no network requests.
Will it delete anything?
Only if you choose Move mode, and then only files it has verified were copied. Copy mode, the default, leaves your originals alone.
What counts as a duplicate?
Byte-for-byte identical content, regardless of file name or location. Resized or re-exported versions of a photo are different files and are not matched yet.
Which copy does it keep?
By default, the newest by modified date. You can switch to oldest, override any group, or settle whole folders at once with folder clones.
My file dates are wrong because my backups reset them.
The viewer can read the real capture date from JPEG photos (EXIF) and sort by it, along with camera model and dimensions. Other formats fall back to the file date.
How big an archive can it handle?
It's built with large archives in mind: tiered fingerprinting, background workers, and chunked hashing for huge files. Scan speed is mostly limited by how fast your drive can be read. Start with a small folder to see how it behaves on your hardware.
Why doesn't it work in Firefox or Safari?
They don't support the browser feature that lets a web page work with real folders on your drive. Chrome and Edge on desktop do.

Yes.
